Business is hard. There is no way around it. If it was easy, everyone would be able to do it and it wouldn't be worth anything. A lot of the reasons why this is difficult for so many is because of all the tradeoffs you inevitably have to make. A person who is more focused on running a business would beat the person who is not. This means giving up things like social events, clubs, classes, friendships, etc., which many are not willing to make. I was one of them. 

In my first quarter of university, I believed I could do it all. I tried clubs, gym, swimming, clubs, grades, social events and startups. However, I soon realized that I was excelling in none of them. My grades were dropping. My mental health was declining. I wasn't able to attend clubs or social events. I just wasn't able to juggle it all. And I came to a quick realization that in order to truly be great at one thing, you have to give up everything else. Aka tradeoffs!

But most people are not willing to make tradeoffs. Most people are so distracted by politics, news, entertainment, video games, TikTok, and everything else that they cannot possibly focus on one thing for long enough to be great at it. I was so preoccupied with being great at everything that I got great at nothing. The first step in running a successful business is to fully come in terms with the fact that you won't be great at everything. You have to be willing to make sacrifices. 

One way of doing this is to write down on paper or typing on a computer all the things you are willing to sacrifice and all the things you are willing to gain from achieving your goal. For example, I made a deliberate choice to sacrifice clubs, gpa, social events, swimming, and everything else in order to run a business. Because no matter what, the person who is more focused at one thing would beat the person who is not. That is the secret ingredient to be successful in any endeavor you wish to undertake.

Focus has two parts to it. The first part is bravery. You got to be willing to make the courageous (riskiest!) decision. Making tradeoffs is a brave decision. Saying no to opportunities or events in the hope of achieving you goals is a brave decisions. When I approach multiple opportunities, I always ask myself: what would be the bravest decision I can make. Starting a business is a brave decision. Saying no to everything that isn't running a business is a brave decision. Meeting new people and potential business partners are a brave decision. You gotta make more brave decisions! 

Brave decisions help for two reasons. First less people are willing to make brave decisions so you automatically get first mover advantage. All the greatest moments that occured in my life came after taking some form of risk, whether it was meeting a random stranger online for a "coffee chat" or getting the guts to make the first cold call. That stranger turned out to be my first paying customer and invaluble mentor to future endeavors. The first cold call, no matter how embarrasing, allowed me to get over the uncertainity and fear of calling strangers. Another reason why bravery pains dividends is that it has infinite upside. If the brave decision works out, it can change the entire trajectory of your life, forever. Getting that first customer changed the perspective of how I approached business, forever. 

Another reason that makes brave decisions so effective is the larger you are as a company or business, the harder it is to make risky decisions. Riskier decisions have more downside then upside, as long as you are a large company. A bigger business has to go through layers of beuracracy (managers, legal, etc) to change the way they operate and make money. You don't! The ability for you to take on more risk will not only allow you to infinitely scale your business, but also stand out from all the other players in the game. Brave decisions have uncapped upside and capped downside. You ought to be willing to make brave decisions! 

The second part of focus is persistence. In order to be focused, you not only have to make the sacrifices but also stick to one thing for a long enough time period. As Tony Stark said it in Endgame, "We are all kinda stubborn". The ability to keep doing the same thing over and over again will put you miles ahead of your competitors. That is also why writing down a Terms and Conditions, and putting it somewhere you can see, will keep you focused and motivated through all the ups and downs that is your business. Once you got both persistance and bravery down, you can achieve almost anything in life, whether it is in business, friendships, dating, job hunting, research, sales or whatever else you end up pursuing. 

Onwards,

Samir
