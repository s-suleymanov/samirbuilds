Business is hard. There is no way around it. If it was easy, everyone would be able to do it and it wouldn't be worth anything. A lot of the reasons why this is difficult for so many is because of all the tradeoffs you inevitably have to make. A person who is more focused on running a business would beat the person who is not. This means giving up things like social events, clubs, classes, games, etc., which many are not willing to make. I was one of them. 

In my first quarter of university, I believed I could do it all. I tried clubs, gym, swimming, clubs, grades, social events and startups. However, I soon realized that I was excelling in none of them. My grades were dropping. My mental health was declining. I wasn't able to attend clubs or social events. I just wasn't able to juggle it all. And I came to a quick realization that in order to truly be great at one thing, you have to give up everything else. Aka tradeoffs!

But most people are not willing to make tradeoffs. Most people are so distracted by politics, news, entertainment, video games, TikTok, and everything else that they cannot possibly focus on one thing for long enough to be great at it. I was so preoccupied with being great at everything that I got great at nothing. The first step in running a successful business is to fully come in terms with the fact that you won't be great at everything. You have to be willing to make tradeoffs.

One way of doing this is to write down on paper or type on a computer all the things you are willing to sacrifice and all the things you are willing to gain from achieving your goal. I called it Terms and Conditions (T&C) because you are essentially making a pact with yourself on what you want to achieve, and more importantly, what you are not going to achieve. In my T&C, I made a deliberate choice to sacrifice clubs, gpa, jobs, hobbies, social events, swimming, and everything else that wasn't running a business. 

Because I knew that no matter what I chose, the person who was more focused would beat the person who was not. The more focused job seeker would get more interviews than me. The more focused swimmer will win more races than me. The more focused business owner would earn more than me. Focus is subtraction. And to subtract means to give up things you otherwise thought were valuable or important. That is why T&C is such a powerful tool, because it reminds you of why you chose this path and what you are willing to give up as a result.

The best NBA player had to give up everything that wasn't basketball. The best chess player had to give up everything that wasn't chess. The best business owner had to give up everything that wasn't business. And the beautiful thing about life is that if you are not willing to make these sacrifices, than that's also your choice. As long as you willing to make the tradeoff which is that you won't be truly great at one thing. In reality, it is impossible to be 100% focused on one thing because even the most focused business owners need to eat, shower, sleep, etc. But the principle still holds true. In order to be good at one thing, you have to give up everything else. Less is more. Focus is subtraction.

Focus has two secret ingredients. The first ingredient is bravery. You got to be willing to make the most courageous decision. Making tradeoffs is a brave decision. Saying no to social events in the hope of achieving you goals is a brave decisions. When I approach multiple opportunities, I always ask myself: what would be the bravest decision I can make. Starting a business is a brave decision. Saying no to everything that isn't running a business is a brave decision. Saying yes to new opportunities is a brave decision. You gotta make more brave decisions and here is why.

Brave decisions help for two reasons. First less people are willing to make brave decisions so you automatically get first mover advantage. All the greatest moments that occured in my life came after taking some form of risk, whether it was meeting a random stranger online for a "coffee chat" or getting the guts to make the first cold call. That stranger turned out to be my first paying customer and invaluble mentor to future endeavors. The first cold call, no matter how embarrasing, allowed me to get over the uncertainity and fear of calling strangers. Another reason why bravery pays dividends is that it has infinite upside. If the brave decision works out, it can change the entire trajectory of your life, forever. Getting that first customer changed the perspective of how I approached business, forever. 

Another reason that makes brave decisions so effective is the larger you are as a company or business, the harder it is to make riskier decisions. For larger orgs, riskier decisions have higher downsides than upsides. A bigger business has to go through layers of bureaucracy (managers, legal, etc) to change the way they operate. The time and capital it takes for them to carry out any sort of change is not worth the loss in revenue and existing user base. But you don't! The ability for you to take on more risk will not only allow you to infinitely scale your business, but also stand out from all the other players in the game. Brave decisions have uncapped upside and capped downside. You ought to be willing to make brave decisions if you want to stand out! 

The second ingredient to focus is persistence. In order to stay focused, you not only have to make sacrifices, but you also have to stick with one thing for a long enough period of time. As Tony Stark said in Endgame, “We are all kinda stubborn.” In many ways, that stubbornness is exactly what persistence requires. The ability to keep doing the same thing over and over again, especially after the excitement wears off, can put you miles ahead of your competitors. That is also why writing down your own Terms and Conditions and putting them somewhere you can see them can be so useful.

What makes persistence interesting is that it can almost seem like the opposite of bravery. Persistence is the refusal to quit when things become repetitive, boring, or mundane, whereas bravery is the willingness to take action despite the possibility of short-term costs. One keeps you on the path; the other gives you a way out. A useful way to think about this is to lean toward persistence when things are working and lean toward bravery when they are not. But business is rarely that black and white. You will have to develop both persistence and bravery, and more importantly, when to use which if you really want to succeed!

Humans are most afraid of two things: uncertainty and delay. If you can provide a way to take on the uncertainty (by being brave) or delay (by being persistent), not only will you be paid, but you will come out as a stronger, more capable individual.
